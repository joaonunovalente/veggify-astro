## Why you'll love this recipe

Peanut butter belongs in the batter, not only on top, and that is the whole idea behind this recipe. Whisked in with the almond milk, it sweetens and enriches the batter so these taste like peanut butter from the inside out. You end up with pancakes that are actually fluffy, because there are no eggs and no melted butter to weigh the batter down.

There is a real amount of whole-wheat flour in here too, which would normally make pancakes heavy. It works because the peanut butter and the sugar keep everything tender, and because you rest the batter so the flour hydrates properly.

Thirty minutes start to finish, one bowl, one skillet, and it scales by doubling the recipe without changing anything else. This is the one I make on Saturday mornings when there are extra people in the house.

## How to make this recipe

Mix the peanut butter with the milk first, before it meets the flour. Straight into the dry ingredients, peanut butter sits in sticky clumps and you end up chasing them around the bowl with a spatula for longer than it takes to cook the pancakes.

Fold, do not stir, and stop the moment the last dry streak disappears. Those lumps in the batter are fine. What you are trying to avoid is working the flour until it develops gluten, which is what makes pancakes dense and chewy.

Then wait. Five minutes of rest is not optional, even though it feels like wasted time. It hydrates the whole-wheat flour and gives the baking powder a head start, so you get more lift from the same amount of leavening.

Watch for the bubbles breaking at the edges and the top turning matte, not for a fixed time. That is the real signal, and it is the difference between a golden pancake and a pale one.

## Substitutions

- Any nut butter works, or use sunflower seed butter for a nut-free version. Almond butter is the sweetest of the bunch.
- For banana pancakes, mash one ripe banana into the wet mixture and reduce the sugar to 2 Tbsp.
- Swap half the flour for oat flour for a softer crumb, or use a 1:1 gluten-free blend cup for cup.
- Add 1/2 cup of chocolate chips, or sliced bananas, to the batter right before the first pancake goes on.
- The batter keeps for 2 days in the fridge. Add a splash of milk before cooking the next round, as it thickens as it rests.
- Try it with savory toppings too: maple syrup and flaky salt is the best way to see how much of that peanut butter is still there.
