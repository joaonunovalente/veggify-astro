## Why you'll love this recipe

Two cocktails, both of them the kind you can make without a shaker, a jigger, or anything you would have to buy first. This is the pair I make when I want someone to actually enjoy their drink rather than stand at my counter taking notes.

The mojito is the friendliest drink there is. Bright, bubbly, and slightly sweet, it looks impressive in the glass and is genuinely hard to mess up. The martini is the one worth learning, because it teaches the thing that separates a good drink from a mixed one: stir properly, with ice, for long enough that the glass frosts over. Three minutes of practice covers both of these forever.

Neither recipe needs anything beyond a few ingredients you can get at a supermarket, and the mojito works in a pitcher if you are making drinks for a group.

## How to make this recipe

Start with the ice, and make more than you think you need. You will use ice to chill the glass, ice to stir with, and ice to serve it over, and running out halfway through is a common way first attempts go wrong.

For the mojito, treat the mint gently. Pressing it just to bruise the leaves releases the oil and smells wonderful. Muddling it hard, which everyone does at first, muddies the drink and makes it taste soapy.

For the martini, the only real technique is stirring until the mixing glass frosts over on the outside. That takes 20 to 30 seconds and it is what properly dilutes and chills the drink. Shaking works too, but it throws a frothy blanket over the top and softens what should be a clear, cold glass.

Chill your serving glasses before you start, in both cases. Ice melts fastest in a warm glass, and for a drink this cold and this carbonated, that matters more than anything else in the method.

## Substitutions

- A 1:1 simple syrup, equal parts sugar and water, is all the mojito needs. Or use 1 tsp of agave nectar and skip the sugar entirely.
- Use whatever citrus is in season. Grapefruit and lime both work well in a mojito.
- For a martini, the ratio is the thing to memorize: 2 parts spirit to 1 part dry vermouth, 3 parts to 1 for a wetter, stronger drink.
- Gin or vodka both belong in a martini. Vodka is the traditional choice, gin is the modern one, and neither one is wrong.
- Add a splash of olive brine to the glass and stir it around first for a saltier, more old-fashioned version.
- Skip the alcohol entirely with a zero-proof spirit and a dry white wine in place of the vermouth. Both drinks work as they are.
