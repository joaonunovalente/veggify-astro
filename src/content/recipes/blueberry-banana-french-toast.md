## Why you'll love this recipe

This is the one I make when there is still a little banana going soft on the counter and about fifteen minutes before everyone needs breakfast. There is no soaking for an hour, no custard to scald, and no blender to clean out. Twenty minutes, one bowl, one pan.

What separates good French toast from great is the custard, and the trick here is putting the banana in it rather than on top. One ripe mashed banana sweetens the whole bowl naturally and gives you a custard that tastes baked rather than just eggy. The cornstarch thickens it slightly so it clings to the bread instead of sliding off into the pan.

Blueberries go on at the very end, while the toast is still hot. Warm berries and warm toast together are what turn this from a nice breakfast into the one people ask about.

## How to make this recipe

Use thick bread. Brioche, sourdough, or a dense whole-grain loaf all hold up; thin sandwich bread falls apart, and you will be fishing it out of the pan in pieces.

Mash the banana properly, right down to the last streak. Lumps of banana that miss the whisk end up as hot spots in the custard. Rest the mixture for five minutes before you dip anything — that is when the cornstarch thickens it and the bread stops absorbing it like a sponge.

Keep the heat at medium-low. Too hot and the outsides scorch before the middle cooks through, which is the classic French toast failure. Cook low and slow, three to four minutes a side, and you get that puffed, custardy center with a properly caramelized crust.

Warm the maple syrup separately in a pan rather than pouring it cold over the toast. It flows better and it will not slide right off.

## Substitutions

- Any ripe fruit works in the custard. Raspberries and diced strawberries both work well, or use extra mashed banana.
- Thick-cut brioche is the best base for richness, but day-old bread is actually better than fresh, since it holds its shape.
- Swap almond milk for oat milk if you want a slightly richer custard, or for soy milk if avoiding nuts.
- For a savory turn, leave out the banana and maple syrup and add 1/2 tsp each of cinnamon and nutmeg, then serve with strawberries and powdered sugar.
- Make a French toast casserole if you are feeding a crowd: soak the slices overnight, then bake at 375 °F (190 °C) for 30 minutes.
