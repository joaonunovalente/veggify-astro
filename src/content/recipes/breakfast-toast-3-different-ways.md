## Why you'll love this recipe

One loaf of bread, three genuinely different breakfasts. This is my answer to the weeknight question of what to make when the fridge has bread, a banana, and nothing else, and it is also just three good toast recipes that happen to take almost no time.

The sweet one is the obvious crowd pleaser: nut butter, banana, maple syrup, a little cinnamon. The savory one is the surprise, with basil pesto and tomatoes, which tastes like a summer lunch and takes exactly as long. And the spicy one, avocado with chili crisp and pickled jalapeños, is the reason people ask me for this file.

All three land on the same foundation: toast the bread until it is genuinely crisp, spread something on it while it is still hot, and finish immediately. Toast waits for nothing.

## How to make this recipe

The one rule that matters for all three is not to let the toast sit once it is dressed. Pesto dulls and darkens, nut butter goes dull against bread that has cooled, and avocado goes dull faster than anything. Have everything sliced and measured before you start toasting.

Get the crust right. A pale slice is a soft slice, and you want it well browned at the edges with a little give in the middle. If your toaster runs small, give it a second pass, or use a skillet.

For the savory version, spread the pesto on the toast while it is still hot. Warm bread absorbs it instead of sitting on top of it, which is the difference between a good toast and a soggy one.

Build the spicy version last and serve it right away. Mashed avocado needs a squeeze of lime and something crunchy to stay interesting, and it does not wait.

## Substitutions

- Any sturdy bread works. Brioche makes all three richer, while a seeded rye pushes the savory version toward an open-faced sandwich.
- Nut butter can be swapped 1:1 for any seed butter, or for sunflower seed butter if nut allergies are a concern.
- For the savory version, pesto is the shortcut, not a requirement. Basil, olive oil, garlic, and lemon take two minutes by hand.
- Add a pinch of chili flakes or a drizzle of honey to the savory version if you want to blur the lines between sweet and spicy.
- Grilled sourdough in a pan with a little olive oil gets you a restaurant-level base for all three.
- Leftover chopped toppings make great lunch: the savory version reheats in a skillet, and the spicy one is good cold with a boiled egg on top.
