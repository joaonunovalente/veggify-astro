## Why you'll love this recipe

This is the recipe for the moment when the strawberries are perfect and you have nowhere to be. There is no cooking, no baking, and nothing that needs more than a bowl and a fork, which is also why it is the only dessert I make in the middle of summer.

Mashing a third of the strawberries is the whole trick. It thickens them into a quick sauce that clings to the yogurt instead of sliding to the bottom of the glass, so every spoonful has fruit in it rather than just yogurt and then, at the end, a puddle of syrup.

Toasting the granola takes three minutes and it is the other thing that makes this taste intentional. Ninety seconds in a dry skillet and the nuts go from soft and pale to properly crunchy and fragrant.

Fifteen minutes, two glasses, and it looks like you did more work than you did.

## How to make this recipe

Dress the berries first and let them sit. The lemon juice keeps the strawberries bright and the few minutes of resting time is when they release enough juice to become a loose sauce rather than dry, separate cubes.

Toast the granola in a dry skillet, not a baking sheet. A sheet of nuts in a hot oven takes twelve minutes and a whole oven to heat; a skillet takes three, and the heat is more even. Watch it, because granola goes from toasted to burnt in under a minute at the end.

Keep the yogurt loose with a stir rather than whipping it. You want it thick enough to hold a layer, but loose enough that it coats the back of the spoon.

Layer with intent and finish with the crunch. The granola and pistachios go on at the very top, after the last layer of yogurt, so they are the first thing you see and the first thing you taste.

## Substitutions

- Use any berry. Raspberries do not need mashing, since they break down on their own.
- Swap the granola for toasted almonds, coconut flakes, or a mix of both for a nut-free or lower-sugar version.
- Any unsweetened plant yogurt works. Thick coconut yogurt is good if you want the parfait to feel more like a dessert.
- Add a tablespoon of chocolate chips or a drizzle of melted chocolate for a more decadent version.
- To turn this into breakfast, swap the maple syrup for a little honey or date syrup and layer in some toasted coconut.
- In winter, swap the berries for 1 cup of roasted rhubarb or poached pear and keep everything else the same.
