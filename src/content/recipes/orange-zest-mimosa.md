## Why you'll love this recipe

A mimosa is one of the easiest drinks to make and one of the easiest to make badly, usually because of heat and glassware rather than the recipe. Both are solved here. Chill the wine, the juice, and the flutes ahead of time, and pour each person their own glass rather than passing a pitcher around the table.

What actually lifts this above the standard one is the zest. Those aromatic oils are the smell of an orange, and they are exactly what most mimosas are missing. Zesting directly over the glass means you get the fragrance without any of the bitter white pith.

Freshly squeezed juice is the other thing. It tastes sharper and more citrusy than anything from a carton, and paired with the sweetness of triple sec it makes the whole drink taste deliberate rather than just boozy.

## How to make this recipe

Keep everything cold. Warm juice and a warm glass are the two reasons a mimosa disappoints, so chill the flutes in the freezer for ten minutes if you are short on time, and remember that a cold bottle in the fridge for a few hours is never enough on its own.

Pour half, taste, adjust, then top. Put the orange juice in first and the sparkling wine on top, so the carbonation stays on the surface where it does the work instead of being stirred through the bottom of the glass.

Zest at the last possible moment. The oils disperse within minutes, so anything done in advance ends up as flecks with no aroma at all. Use a microplane if you have one and stop at the outer colored layer, because the white pith underneath is where the bitterness lives.

Stir exactly once, gently. Every hard stir knocks the bubbles out of a mimosa, and you will not get them back.

## Substitutions

- Orange juice and sparkling wine in equal parts is the classic ratio. For a drier version, use two parts prosecco to one part juice.
- Skip the liqueur entirely for a lighter drink, or use a small splash of triple sec to round it out.
- Blood oranges make this stunning in winter, when they are tart enough to need the liqueur anyway.
- Add a splash of fresh pomegranate or cranberry juice for color and a sharper edge.
- Make it a bellini with 1 part peach or guava purée blended into 2 parts sparkling wine, no juice at all.
- For the zero-proof version, use a sparkling white grape or elderflower pressé in place of the wine.
