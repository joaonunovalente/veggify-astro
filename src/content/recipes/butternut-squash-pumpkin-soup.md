## Why you'll love this recipe

There are a dozen ways to make butternut squash soup, and almost all of them start by boiling raw squash in stock for an hour. This one starts differently: you brown the squash in a skillet first, and that six minutes of prep is where the whole flavor lives.

Once squash has caramelized, everything else is easy. Pumpkin purée thickens and sweetens the pot, coconut milk rounds it out, and the maple syrup, ginger, garlic, and sage push it firmly toward the holidays without needing cream. It is the sort of soup that tastes like it simmered all day, because it did.

The other thing I like is how forgiving it is. A slow cooker does not need supervision, the purée covers for any squash that went slightly overcooked, and it freezes beautifully. This is the soup I make in October and then reheat my way through February.

## How to make this recipe

Start by getting the squash cut evenly, about one-inch cubes. Small and large pieces cook at different rates, and you want them all to dissolve at the same time rather than leaving you with chunks in the purée.

Brown the cubes in a hot skillet with the oil, in a single layer, and resist moving them for the first few minutes. Those golden patches you get are the difference between a soup that tastes like squash and one that tastes like fall.

Everything else goes into the slow cooker at once. Do not add the sage yet — eight hours of heat turns it gray and flat — but stir it in at the very end.

Blend until there is no texture left at all, then taste before you serve. Pumpkin and squash are sweet, which means the salt you used during prep no longer reads as enough. Add it in small increments until the sweetness has something to sit against.

## Substitutions

- No slow cooker? Bake at 325 °F (165 °C) in a covered Dutch oven for about 90 minutes, then blend.
- Skip the pumpkin entirely and double the squash if you want to keep it in season. Add it in the last hour so it does not overcook.
- For a lighter soup, use unsweetened plant milk instead of coconut milk and skip the maple syrup.
- Swap sage for thyme, or add a small piece of ginger, peeled and sliced, which infuses more cleanly than ground.
- A tablespoon of curry powder and a cup of red lentils turns this into a dahlier, more filling bowl. Add both with the broth.
