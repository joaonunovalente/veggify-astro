## Why you'll love this recipe

A chickpea salad has to do a lot with very little, and most versions rely on the pantry for all the flavor. This one crisps the chickpeas in a skillet first, which takes about five minutes and turns them from soft and beige into something with a crackle and a nutty, almost meaty flavor. It is the single change that makes this salad feel like a meal instead of a side.

The dressing is where the real work is. Lemon tahini is one of those things that sounds like it will be tricky and is not, as long as you add water slowly. It seizes into a stiff paste the second you stir it, then loosens into the silkiest, most addictive dressing you will make this month. The maple syrup is there to round out the acidity, not to sweeten it.

It is also the lunch I pack on the first warm day of the year: 30 minutes, one pan, one bowl, and it holds up if you make it slightly ahead.

## How to make this recipe

Make the dressing first, then let it sit while you cook. It only improves in the time it takes the chickpeas to crisp, and you want it pourable, not so thick it sits in globules.

Drain and rinse the chickpeas and pat them fairly dry before they go into the pan. Wet chickpeas steam instead of brown. Give them three to four minutes without stirring — that undisturbed time is what builds the crust — then toss and give them two minutes more.

Salt them in the pan while they are hot, along with the parsley, so the seasoning sticks.

Let the chickpeas cool for five minutes before the dressing goes on, then toss everything once, firmly, using your hands. Two full turns is all it takes. Taste before you serve: chickpeas on their own are bland, and this dressing is seasoned enough that you often need less than you planned.

## Substitutions

- For a shortcut, skip the skillet entirely and use two cans of roasted chickpeas, or one can each of chickpeas and white beans.
- Tahini thickens as it sits. Loosen leftovers with water rather than more lemon, or they will turn sharp.
- Sun-dried tomatoes work in place of fresh here, so use 1/3 cup of the oil-packed kind and add them with the chickpeas.
- Add a handful of arugula or baby spinach at the end for a more substantial salad.
- Swap parsley for cilantro if you like the dressing more herbal. It is a good match with the cumin in there.
- This dressing is also excellent over roasted vegetables, grain bowls, or as a dip for warm pita.
