## Why you'll love this recipe

Steak fries should be thin, sharp, and shatteringly crisp — the kind you need nothing more than a bowl of dip for. An air fryer gets you there in about 15 minutes, with no deep-frying oil to heat up, cool down, or find a jar for.

Two details do most of the work. Soaking the cut potatoes strips away the surface starch that turns fries into mush, and a light coating of cornstarch gives the outside a glassy, brittle crunch once the hot air hits it. What is left is even browning, because the fan circulates from every side, so you are not flipping half a batch to find the pale pieces.

Make them for a Tuesday dinner with whatever is in the fridge, or make a double batch, because they disappear faster than you expect.

## How to make this recipe

Everything hinges on prep. Scrub the potatoes but leave the skins on — they crisp up and hold the shape of the fry — and cut them all to the same thickness so the batch finishes together. If your air fryer runs hot, err a little thinner.

Soak the sticks for 10 minutes, then drain and dry them thoroughly. This is the step people skip, and it is the step that matters. Any surface moisture boils off inside the basket and stops the browning dead.

Toss with the oil and seasoning in your largest bowl so every fry gets coated, adding the cornstarch last so it sticks to the oil. A tablespoon of oil is plenty; more just makes them greasy.

Cook in a single layer, in batches if you need to, and shake the basket once. Season with flaky salt while they are hot so it dissolves into the surface instead of sitting on top.

## Substitutions

- Sweet potatoes work well and come out a little sweeter. Cut them a touch thicker and add 3 to 4 extra minutes.
- Yukon Golds are the most forgiving potato here: no soaking needed, and they brown beautifully on their own.
- For cajun fries, swap the smoked paprika for a doubling of the chili powder and black pepper and add 1 tsp of dried thyme.
- Toss the finished fries with garlic powder and chopped parsley for a quick dinner-herb version.
- Keep them warm in a 200 °F (95 °C) oven rather than the microwave, which softens the crust on the way to the table.
- A splash of malt vinegar over the hot fries turns these into proper diner-style chips.
