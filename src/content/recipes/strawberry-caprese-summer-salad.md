## Why you'll love this recipe

Caprese is a salad with three ingredients in it: tomatoes, mozzarella, basil. This is the same idea built for strawberries, and it works because strawberries are a fruit that behaves like a tomato — sweet, acidic, and completely changed by good oil, salt, and fresh herbs.

The balsamic and basil combination is what makes it feel Italian rather than just fruity. There is no cooking involved and nothing takes longer than ten minutes of letting the strawberries sit and get juicy, which makes this the salad I bring when I do not want to cook but still want something that looks like effort.

Toasted almonds add the crunch that a Caprese never has, and they are the reason this does not feel like the same salad twice. Keep them out of the bowl until the last second, though, because they go soft fast.

## How to make this recipe

Dress the strawberries first, on their own. Salt them with a little olive oil and wait ten minutes. They collapse slightly, release their juice, and go from a firm berry into something juicy and fragrant, and that juice seasons the whole bowl for free.

Tear the basil, do not chop it. Chopping bruises the leaves and the stems taste grassy in a salad like this, so hold them just above the stem and pull. If the basil is at all wilted, drop it in a bowl of ice water for five minutes and it will perk right back up.

Soak the onion. Ten seconds sounds pointless and it is not, since it takes the sharp edge off without leaving you with a bland, watery vegetable.

Use a wide bowl and your hands. Everything is delicate, and tossing with your fingers is gentler and faster than any utensil. Keep the almonds for last and dress them at the table.

## Substitutions

- Balsamic glaze is a nice finish, but you can leave it out or add a spoonful of fig jam for the same sweet-sour note.
- For a more substantial salad, add arugula or baby spinach and a handful of quinoa.
- Peaches, nectarines, or sliced plums all work here in place of strawberries.
- Swap the almonds for pistachios, walnuts, or pumpkin seeds if you need this nut-free.
- Toasted sourdough croutons turn it into a full lunch, and are better than any store-bought crouton.
- If basil is out of season, mint or a little finely chopped mint and parsley together works in a pinch.
